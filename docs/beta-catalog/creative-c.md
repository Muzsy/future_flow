## Chapter 1 — Room for the Unfinished

2026-10-07 to 2027-04-07

The first piece stops before you know how to finish it. An ordinary moment, roughly caught; enough to begin, not enough yet to stand on its own. In November, you leave it unfinished rather than let writing claim every spare hour. Two half-hour sessions a week are the shape you give this attempt to recover something beyond earning a living. Their smallness leaves room for the rest of your life.

Then the routine becomes uneven. A missed session can make the next one feel burdened before it starts: there is the writing itself, and then the imagined obligation to catch up. You return to the next short session without adding hours to repay the absence. By January, you have two rough pieces. Neither makes the interruptions disappear, but both exist because returning does not require a grand restart.

In March, the work changes in a quieter way. Revising the first piece, you cut an explanation and let the scene carry what you had tried to spell out. The remaining words have more space around them. You do not have to begin again to make something better; you can work with what is already there. Alongside that first revision, you draft a third piece. For the first time, a small collection is a possibility made of actual work rather than an intention.

At the six-month mark, all three pieces are still drafts. Your employment and finances have not had to change to make room for them. That matters, even if it is less visible than a finished collection. You wanted writing to become part of your life, not a reason to put the rest of it under strain.

Now the same modest practice could take several directions. You could concentrate on these three pieces and revise them into a private first collection: a bounded task, though one without outside feedback for now. The local writing group is still accepting new members. Pursuing that opportunity and preparing a piece could make room for another perspective, but it would use time you might otherwise spend revising alone; attendance, fit, and useful feedback remain unknown. Or you could attempt two more pieces before deciding what belongs together, giving curiosity more room while putting completion further off.

Three unfinished pieces make the choice concrete. Finishing them would give your small beginning a shape. Seeking feedback might help you see the work differently. Continuing to explore might reveal what you most want to write. What should your practice prioritize next?

## Chapter 3 — A Place to Stop

By its end, the preparation is complete. That is not the same as sending the request, attending the group, or hearing back; none of those steps has been established. Still, the first piece has become something you can ask about specifically, rather than something you must defend whole.

It does give you a revised version to evaluate—a task with edges, rather than one that keeps expanding whenever you look at it.
